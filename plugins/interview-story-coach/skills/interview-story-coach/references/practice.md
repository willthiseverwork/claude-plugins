# Practising an interview

## Choose the interview lens

Use the stated stage when available. Otherwise ask one short question about who the interview is with and what the conversation is meant to assess. The same role can require a different answer at different stages.

| Stage | What the interviewer is likely deciding | Useful practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiter | Is the person plausibly qualified and practically available? | Introduction, proof anchors, motivation, work model, and high-risk gaps. |
| Hiring manager | Can they make the required decisions and operate the work? | Cases, trade-offs, priorities, mechanisms, and collaboration. |
| Founder/executive | Do they show judgment and fit the mandate? | Business outcomes, decision rights, stopped bets, and unresolved mandate questions. |
| Functional depth | Do they have the exact operating depth required? | Separate hands-on execution, system design, leadership, and specialist collaboration. |
| Final/values | How do they behave under pressure and work with others? | Disagreement, feedback, failure, resilience, and two-way qualification. |

## Run the loop

1. Ask one realistic question.
2. Wait for the user's answer.
3. Give focused feedback: what landed, what is missing or overstated, and one change to try.
4. Ask one probe or invite a retry.

The next question should depend on the latest answer. Examples of useful probes:

- “What told you that this was the highest-priority problem?”
- “Which part was your decision, and which part did the team deliver?”
- “What did you stop or deprioritise to make room for that?”
- “How was the result measured, and over what period?”
- “What would you do differently now?”

## Feedback standard

Make feedback actionable and evidence-led. Prefer “state the constraint before the activity so the trade-off is visible” to “be more strategic.” Flag an unsupported result, unclear ownership, or a missing boundary directly. Do not reward polished language that makes an answer less true.

For an industry mismatch, ask one question that exposes the difference in customer, buying process, economics, risk, or operating model. Then help the user draw a bounded parallel or state the gap.
