# Fictional example: turning notes into a case

All people, companies, and figures below are invented. They demonstrate how to preserve ownership and limits, not a result to copy or a conversion benchmark.

**Role requirement:** Improve activation for a self-serve B2B analytics product.

**Raw notes from a fictional candidate:** “At Northstar Metrics, trials were signing up but few teams connected a data source. I noticed the first dashboard was empty. I proposed an import guide and a concierge setup option. Design made the guide, engineering changed the connection screen, and support ran the sessions. I led the problem definition and weekly review. Connected-workspace rate went from 31% to 43% in six weeks. I don't know whether it changed paid conversion yet.”

**Case:**

- **Situation / constraint:** New trials could create accounts but often saw an empty product. The team had limited engineering capacity.
- **Personal ownership:** The candidate diagnosed the empty-dashboard problem, proposed two activation paths, and ran the weekly review. They did not build the guide or ship the product change.
- **Mechanism:** The team prioritised setup help over a broader redesign, then compared connection completion weekly.
- **Result:** Connected-workspace rate rose from 31% to 43% during the six-week change period. Paid-conversion impact is unknown.
- **Boundary:** This is direct activation diagnosis and cross-functional leadership; it is not proof of owning all product implementation or of a conversion lift.

**Concise answer shape:** “I improved an activation bottleneck by focusing on the first moment teams could see value. I diagnosed that a blank dashboard followed account creation, proposed a guided import and optional concierge setup, and led the weekly review while design, engineering, and support delivered their parts. Connected workspaces rose from 31% to 43% over six weeks. I would still validate downstream conversion before calling it a revenue result.”
